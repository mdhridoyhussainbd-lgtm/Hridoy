What a useful monthly social media report actually shows

Most social reports are twelve pages of charts that nobody reads. A useful one fits on a single page and answers one question: is this bringing us patients, and what do we change next month?

Part 01Four numbers, in order

Each number moves a person one step closer to booking. I report them as a chain, because a drop at any step tells you exactly where to look.

  • Reach: how many different people saw your content. Is the brand being seen at all?
  • Saves and shares: how many people found a post useful enough to keep or pass on. This is the best sign that the content is good.
  • Profile visits: how many people got curious about who you are.
  • Link clicks or bookings: how many took the action that actually matters to the practice.

Part 02The top three posts, and why

Not just which posts did best, but a one-line guess at why: "short answer to a common fear", "real team member on camera", "posted the morning after a news story". That turns a report into next month's plan.

Part 03One thing we'll change

Every report ends with a single, specific change, such as "move Reels to Tuesday evenings" or "turn the most-saved carousel into a Reel". One change per month is something you can actually test.

What I leave out: follower count on its own, likes without context, and "impressions" without reach. They look good in a chart but rarely help anyone decide anything.

How often to look

Monthly is the right rhythm for most clinics. Weekly numbers jump around too much to read, and a quarter is too long to wait before fixing something that isn't working.
