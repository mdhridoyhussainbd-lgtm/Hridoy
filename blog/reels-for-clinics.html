Why your clinic needs Reels, even if your doctor hates the camera

Short video is where many patients now meet a practice for the first time, before they ever see the website. The good news: you don't need a confident presenter to do it well.

Why video, and why short

A 30-second clip can show what a still post can't: the room, the voice, the way your team explains things. That's what makes a stranger feel they already know you before they book. Instagram, TikTok and YouTube all push short vertical video to people who don't follow you yet, which makes it the cheapest way to be discovered.

Five formats that don't need a face

01Hands and a model

Film only the provider's hands explaining something on an anatomy model or a printed scan. It feels expert and calm, and nobody has to look into the lens.

02Voice over b-roll

Record the explanation as audio on a phone, then cover it with clips of the clinic, equipment or a whiteboard sketch. Many doctors are much more relaxed talking than being filmed.

03Text-on-screen FAQ

One common question, answered in three or four short lines of on-screen text over a simple background clip. No voice needed at all.

04A day at the practice

Opening up, preparing a room, the team's morning huddle (with nothing patient-related in frame). It makes the place feel human and familiar.

05Meet the team

Nurses, receptionists and coordinators are often happier on camera than the doctor, and patients meet them first anyway.

The 30-minute monthly routine: I send a shot list the week before ("10 seconds of hands on the spine model", "the waiting room in morning light"). Someone films it on a phone in one sitting. I edit it into four to six Reels with a hook in the first second, subtitles and a clean cover.

Subtitles are not optional

A large share of people scroll with the sound off. Every Reel should make sense with no audio: burned-in subtitles, a text hook on the first frame, and a clear last line telling people what to do next.
