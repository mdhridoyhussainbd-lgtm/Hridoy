5 rules for health posts that build trust

People read health content when they're worried. One overpromising post can undo a year of good work. These are the five rules I follow for every clinic and health brand I post for.

Rule 01Only make claims you can back up

If a post says a treatment "works", someone should be able to ask "according to what?" and get an answer. Keep a source for every factual claim: a guideline, a published study, or your provider's own clinical experience, clearly labelled as such.

Words like cure, guaranteed, 100% safe and miracle almost never survive that question. Replace them with what is actually true: "helps many patients", "can reduce symptoms", "talk to us about whether it's right for you".

Rule 02Protect patients first

Never post anything that could identify a patient (a face, a name, a chart on screen, a recognisable story) without their written consent. In the US, clinics covered by HIPAA need a signed authorisation before using patient information in marketing. Other countries have their own privacy laws.

A simple habit: before filming in the clinic, check every screen, whiteboard and desk in the frame. Most privacy slips happen in the background, not the foreground.

Rule 03No miracle before-and-afters

Before-and-after content feels persuasive, which is exactly why regulators and platforms watch it closely. In the US, the FTC expects testimonials to reflect results people can generally expect, or to clearly say they don't. Meta's ad rules also restrict before-and-after images for things like weight loss.

Educational content usually builds more trust anyway. "Here's what recovery looks like week by week" beats "look at this amazing result".

Rule 04Write like a good doctor talks

Patients don't search for "glycated haemoglobin". They search for "what does my A1c mean". Use the words your patients use, explain one idea per post, and keep sentences short. If a term is necessary, explain it right away.

Rule 05End every post with one clear action

A post that educates but doesn't say what to do next leaves people stuck. Pick one action per post: save this, send it to someone who needs it, or book a consultation. Don't ask for all three at once.

This is practical guidance from my work, not legal advice. Advertising and privacy rules differ by country and specialty (for example the FTC and HIPAA in the US, or the ASA and GMC guidance in the UK). When in doubt, check with your compliance lead.
